## Scope and sequence

Tom's latest architecture ruling moves Blender authoring to its own cluster workload so its image, dependencies, and process can be upgraded without restarting dev-env. He also has a server capable of dual RTX 3090s and smaller GPUs in the cluster. That capacity is available to investigate; no GPU assignment or performance trial has been completed.

1. Build and deploy a dedicated, pinned Blender authoring service through `haynes-ops` and Flux. Colocate the graphical Blender session, Xvfb/software graphics, and matching MCP bridge in the dedicated pod. Expose streamable HTTP at `/mcp` on an internal Service; keep the raw add-on socket on loopback. Pin FFmpeg/ffprobe, glTF Transform, and the Khronos validator with the authoring toolchain. CPU/software rendering is the setup smoke baseline.
2. Give the service its own persistent workspace, independent of the dev-env home PVC. Define controlled artifact upload/download for references, `.blend` masters, screenshots, and exports; an agent-local path is not automatically a remote path. Constrain the Service and artifact transfer with Cilium policy, disable bridge telemetry, and keep the command-capable endpoint internal to authorized authoring clients.
3. Test the dedicated image and live Service under its intended non-root, read-only-rootfs and writable-storage constraints. Verify remote MCP initialization/tool listing, scene commands, screenshot bytes, save/reopen, GLB export/import/validation, synthetic audio encoding, and artifact transfer with matching checksums. Restart the authoring pod and reopen the persistent scene. Confirm dev-env remains running through the authoring rollout. Offline results from the earlier image are useful precedent, not proof of this deployment.
4. After a stable endpoint is deployed and verified, finalize the held [dev-env registration PR #2833](https://github.com/thaynes43/haynes-ops/pull/2833) to register the remote `/mcp` endpoint for both agents, with no Blender image bump. Under the current ConfigMap/reloader arrangement this initial registration still rolls dev-env: Tom merges that separate held draft at a natural break. In a fresh session, verify the generated registrations and live MCP/artifact loop. Later Blender service upgrades use ordinary checked GitOps PRs and do not require a dev-env restart.
5. Keep GPU render Jobs and a separate audio-model service as later authoring options. Inventory the offered GPUs, scheduling/driver support, available VRAM, and workload needs before choosing hardware. CPU baseline checks do not establish acceptable production render or audio generation speed; dual GPUs do not imply pooled model memory or supported multi-GPU inference.
6. Resolve the proposed self-hosted Stable Audio Small-SFX trial or hosted ElevenLabs alternative under DESIGN-008. Self-hosting needs granted model access, accepted terms, protected download credentials/egress, persistent model storage, and a separately pinned service or Job environment. Measure quality, time, and peak memory on the selected CPU/GPU. No account, subscription, terms acceptance, model download, or provider selection is assumed. Record explicit owner deferral if the audio trial is moved beyond this checkpoint.
7. Dispatch new Astra development agents with `fork_turns: "none"`, a self-contained work order, task worktree, required docs, stable contracts, remote tool/artifact details, and explicit acceptance evidence. Use the verified dependency inventory to begin PLAN-002; final asset promotion still needs Tom's review.

## Completion

Complete when authoring tools are live and their checks pass, the audio trial has either verified its selected route or has a clearly recorded owner-deferred scope, and the fresh-context development handoff is self-contained. A green image build alone does not prove the remote MCP connection, artifact transfer, or persistence. A held registration draft is not a connected dependency; GPU capacity alone does not complete an audio trial.
