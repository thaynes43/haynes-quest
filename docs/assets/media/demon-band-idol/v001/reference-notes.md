## Design intent

A clean, sparkly demon boy-band idol, caught mid-performance and very pleased with himself. He is a cute lavender imp in a teal-and-gold stage jacket. He holds a silver stage mic up beside his chin in his right hand and plants his left fist on his hip. His weight rests on his right leg, his head tilts toward the mic, and he throws the audience a big wink over an open grin with star-shaped blush. The age band is 4–6, so he reads as glam and goofy first and "demon" only through cute cues: little gold horns, pointy ears, lavender skin and a spade-tipped tail. He has no fangs, claws, weapons, glowing eyes or angry face.

Silhouette, from top to bottom:

1. **Two little gold candy horns** rise through the hair. They are short and chubby and curve gently in, with blunt round tips. They are the highest point, at 1.40 m.
2. **Swoopy midnight idol hair.** A big side-swept fringe crests at 1.33 m and sweeps across the forehead toward his right. It is dip-dyed hot pink at the two swoop ends, which tuck against his right temple. Plain sideburn tufts sit in front of the ears. At the back, broad overlapping nape points sit under swept-back layers.
3. **A big round lavender head** (0.28 m wide, about 26% of his height, scaled up on purpose for a cute read) with a gently pointed chin. The face has three parts:
   - the left eye is big and open, with a magenta iris, a sparkle highlight and a lash flick;
   - the right eye is a happy wink arc with two lashes;
   - below them sit a lopsided open grin with a pink tongue, a pink five-point star on each cheek, and a button nose.
4. **Pointy ears** with pink insides. A little gold star earring dangles from the left ear.
5. **The shoulder line:** gold epaulettes with fringe, each carrying three sparkle stars (gold, hot pink and white). This is the widest part of the upper body, at 0.48 m.
6. **The stage jacket:** a cropped teal military-style idol jacket. It has a gold stand-up collar, a white shirt V with gold lapel piping, three gold buttons and a gold hem. A **hot-pink sash** runs from his right shoulder across the chest to a **bow at his left hip**, with gold-tipped tails and a gold star brooch where it crosses the chest. Two short gold-tipped swallowtail flaps hang at the back.
7. **The pose:** his right elbow is out and his forearm raised, with the fist wrapped around the mic. A pink star charm hangs from the mic handle. His left elbow is out wide, with the fist on his hip. The two arms leave clear triangles of negative space on both sides.
8. **Legs and feet:** slim midnight stage trousers with a gold glitter side stripe, and chunky white high-top sneakers with hot-pink soles, collar rims and laces, plus a gold patch on the outside of each shoe. The right foot is planted, and the left is turned out.
9. **The tail:** a little lavender demon tail curls out from under the swallowtail and up on his right side, ending in a hot-pink spade. It reads clearly in the side, back and three-quarter views, and peeks beside the right leg in the front view.

## What makes it original

- **No copied names, text, logos, faces, costumes or songs.** The film's demons wear dark stage outfits and traditional hats. We use neither: this idol has a teal-and-gold jacket, a pink sash and no hat. The name stays generic ("demon band idol"). There is no lettering or logo anywhere.
- **Its own design.** Our own choices include the lavender imp skin, gold candy horns, dip-dyed swoop, star blush, sparkle epaulettes, spade tail and the wink-and-mic pose. None comes from a model sheet.
- **Not a likeness.** The lavender skin, horns and cartoon proportions keep him clearly away from any real idol's face or look.
- **Its own palette,** from our storybook brief. It complements the Besties' pink-and-black look on the same stage without matching it.
- **Kid-safe.** The horns have blunt round tips 0.028 m across. There are no pointed teeth, claws or weapons. The mic is his only prop, and his expression is a friendly wink and grin. The tail tip is heart-shaped.

## Orientation and scale conventions

The blockout uses the WO111 authoring convention so the modeling author can keep it:

- Blender +Z is up and the character faces Blender +Y, which becomes glTF +Y up / −Z forward on export.
- The root is floor-centred at the origin, and the character's right is +X.
- The whole head group hangs from one empty, `Head tilt pivot`, at the neck (0, 0, 0.975 m). That group is the head, face, ears, earring, hair and horns. The pivot carries the 6° roll, the −5° yaw, a 1.08 uniform scale and a 12 mm drop.
- Zero the pivot's rotation for a rest pose, but keep its scale.

The sheet shows four linked-data copies of one master at exactly the same orthographic scale, rotated 180° (front), −90° (side, showing his right and the mic), 0° (back) and −135° (three-quarter). The height marker has 5 cm ticks with labels every 25 cm, plus dashed guides. The frame is sized from the figure height, which fills about 45% of the sheet height, matching the bin-chicken sheet.

## Notes for the later modeling author

These are suggestions only; the coordinator's review of this sheet decides.

- **Budget:** the blockout is about 95k triangles of primitives, lathes and sweeps, far over budget. The final model must meet the WO099 contract:
  - ≤ 15k triangles, ≤ 2 materials and one ≤ 1024 atlas;
  - one skin with ≤ 4 influences per vertex;
  - a ≤ 2 MiB GLB;
  - exactly the clips `idle`, `move`, `attack`, `hit` and `defeat`.
- **Paint rather than model the small details.** Put the eyes, wink, brows, lashes, grin, blush stars, shirt V, lapel piping, buttons, glitter stripes, laces and shoe patches into the atlas.
- **Keep as geometry** the parts that carry the silhouette:
  - the horns, hair swoop and ears;
  - the epaulettes, with their sparkle stars merged into one or two simple plates each;
  - the sash bow and swallowtail;
  - the mic, the tail and its spade.

  The epaulette fringe can become a painted band or a few simple fins.
- **Thin parts:** the tail (about 0.03 m thick) and the ear tips are thin. Keep them readable at gameplay distance, perhaps slightly thicker than in the blockout.
- **Rig:** a humanoid skeleton:
  - a hip root, a 3-bone spine, a neck and a head bone, with the horns, ears and earring weighted rigidly to the head;
  - a 2-bone chain in the fringe swoop so it can bounce and flop;
  - each arm with upper arm, forearm and hand, and the mic rigidly parented to the right hand bone;
  - each leg with thigh, shin, foot and toe;
  - a 3–4 bone tail;
  - optionally one bone per sash-bow tail.
- **Pose:** the head tilt, the raised mic arm, the hand on the hip, the turned-out left foot and the weight shift are design poses. Build the rest pose with the head straight, the arms in a relaxed A-pose with the mic in the right hand, and both feet forward. Then recreate the performance pose in `idle`.
- **Possible acting.** A chapter with one ordinary identity uses it in all four slots, so the "trio" of DESIGN-026 could later come from palette swaps of this one model (for example, jacket and sash colors). That is a coordinator decision, not part of this sheet.
  - `idle`: a confident on-the-beat bounce, a shoulder shimmy that jiggles the sparkles, a mic twirl, a blink back into the wink, and the tail swaying.
  - `move`: a boy-band side-step groove with a little shoulder roll. The sash bow and swallowtail bounce, and the tail wags.
  - `attack` (2.0 s, contact at about 1.25 s):
    1. **Wind-up, 0–0.7 s:** he pulls the mic in to his chest, leans back and lifts his chin. The hair swoop bounces and the sparkles shake.
    2. **Build-up, 0.7–1.1 s:** a quick quarter-spin in place (the root stays put) into a lean toward the player.
    3. **Contact, about 1.25 s:** a big "high note". He thrusts the mic out at arm's length, and his free hand pops up into a heart gesture. Any sound-wave or star-note puff at contact is a runtime effect, not model geometry.
    4. **Recovery, 1.25–2.0 s:** a hair flip, a wink, and back to hand-on-hip.
  - `hit`: he stumbles back a step with a round "ow" mouth. The hair swoop flops over both eyes, and the sparkles and tail shake.
  - `defeat`: a dramatic idol swoon. He sits down hard with his legs splayed and his hair flopped over his face. The mic droops in a limp hand and one last sparkle tilts. The pose is held.

## Provenance

- **Authoring:** Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) through the cluster Blender MCP service on instance 1, `blender-authoring` (Blender 4.5.13 LTS), under Tom's September 26 ruling.   The scripts are adapted from the bin-chicken, inator-monster, mischief-kitten, rival-mayor and magic-house v001 sheet scripts, and the sheet format matches them.
- **Iteration record:** four design passes. Each one was checked against the half-size sheet preview and close-ups:
  1. The first pass had tall, outward-curving horns with pink rings, thin pink-tipped strands dangling beside the face, and a stair-stepped hair-cap edge. The title also ran into the palette row.
  2. The head was scaled 1.08 about the neck pivot. The horns became short, chubby and blunt, and the rings were removed. The mic moved closer to the chin, the sparkles became more concave twinkles, the ears got fuller, the swoop ends were lowered, the dangling lock was removed, and the title now fits beside the palette.
  3. The hair cap was rebuilt as a grid that follows the hairline, which cleaned up the edge. The pink dip-dye stayed on the front swoop only, the sideburns became plain, the head was lowered 12 mm, and the eye whites got more room.
  4. From the back, the pink-tipped crown and nape strands read as fingers gripping the head. They were replaced with broad plain nape points and swept-back layers. The fringe tips were tucked against the temple, and the swallowtail was narrowed with a gap for the tail.

  `preview/sheet-preview.png` is the last half-size iteration preview; `reference-sheet.png` is the final render of the same scene.

The scene was claimed on instance 1 from the released inator-monster v001 scene. `claim-checkpoint.blend` is the recovery copy taken at claim time.
