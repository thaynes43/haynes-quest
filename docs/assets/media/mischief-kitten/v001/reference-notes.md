## Design intent

This kitten is a small troublemaker who works for the rival mayor and copies him. It sneaks up on tiptoe, wiggles and pounces, and it looks pleased with itself the whole time. The age band is 2–5, so it has to read as cute first and naughty second. That means a huge round head, big shiny eyes, soft round paws and a tongue poking out. There are no claws, no teeth and no angry face.

Silhouette, from top to bottom:

1. **Two big ears and a tiny tilted top hat.** The right ear stands straight up and the left ear is cocked outward, so the top of the head is lopsided on purpose. The hat sits between them, tipped toward the kitten's left. Hat and right ear both reach about 0.92 m.
2. **Oversized round head**, about 0.47 m wide, nearly half the kitten's height. Whiskers stick out to 0.60 m across, well past the cheeks, and they are the widest read in the front view.
3. **Question-mark tail** swung up on the kitten's right side, with a hooked cream tip at about 0.88 m. It shows beside the head in the front view and as a clear hook in the side view.
4. **Short bean body** with a gadget backpack on top. The chest is higher than the rump, and the haunches are bunched as if it is about to pounce.
5. **Sneaky tiptoe:** the right forepaw is lifted and curled, with pink toe beans facing the viewer. The other three paws stand on the floor in cream "socks".

## What makes it original

- **No copied names, text, logos, faces or costumes.** The kitten carries no lettering or emblem: the collar has a plain bell, and neither the hat band nor the pack has a badge. We avoid the paw-print-on-shield idea, and the name stays generic ("mischief kitten").
- **Its own palette.** Lilac-grey tabby fur with plum-lilac stripes and cream markings, plus the rival mayor's plum, honey, raspberry and teal. These colors come from our storybook brief and our own boss, not from any show.
- **Our own combination of shapes.** The mini stovepipe hat, the spring-and-propeller pack, the question-mark tail swung to one side, the cocked ear and the tiptoe pose are our choices, and none comes from a model sheet.
- **Kid-safe.** No claws, teeth or weapons. The mischief is a pounce and a smug grin, and the tongue-out face keeps it silly.

## Orientation and scale conventions

The blockout uses the WO111 authoring convention so the modeling author can keep it: Blender +Z up and the character facing Blender +Y, which becomes glTF +Y up / −Z forward on export. The root is floor-centred at the origin, and the character's right is +X.

The sheet shows four linked-data copies of one master at exactly the same orthographic scale, rotated 180° (front), −90° (side, facing right), 0° (back) and −135° (three-quarter). The height marker has 5 cm ticks with labels every 25 cm, plus dashed guides.

## Notes for the later modeling author

These are suggestions only; the coordinator's review of this sheet decides.

- **Budget:** the blockout is about 66k triangles of primitives, lathes and curves, far over budget. The final model must meet the WO099 contract: ≤ 15k triangles, ≤ 2 materials, one ≤ 1024 atlas, one skin with ≤ 4 influences, ≤ 2 MiB GLB, and exactly the clips `idle`, `move`, `attack`, `hit` and `defeat`. An ordinary this small should come in well under the cap.
- **Paint rather than model the small details.** Tabby stripes, the toe grooves, the mouth line and the brow tufts can go into the atlas. Whiskers could be thin cards or a baked texture, but keep them readable at gameplay distance, because they carry the "cat" read from the front.
- **Rig:** use a quadruped skeleton with a spine, neck and head, four legs, a 5–6 bone tail chain, and ear bones for flicks. Weight the hat rigidly to a head-child bone and the pack to the spine. The antenna could get one bone so it can wobble.
- **Pose:** the raised forepaw, the cocked ear and the tail swing are design poses. Build the rest pose with four paws down and a neutral tail, then recreate the tiptoe in `idle`.
- **Possible acting:**
  - `idle`: tiptoe paw-lift, tail tip flicks, a smug ear twitch.
  - `move`: a bouncy trot, with the pack and antenna jiggling.
  - `attack` (about 2.0 s): a pounce wind-up where it crouches low and wiggles its bottom (the propeller starts spinning), then it springs forward and bops with both forepaws at about 1.25 s contact, landing on its belly.
  - `hit`: the hat pops up, the fur puffs and the ears go flat.
  - `defeat`: it rolls onto its back, legs up, with the hat over its eyes and the tongue out. The pose is held.
